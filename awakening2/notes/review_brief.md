# Brief for independent reviewers (given to a fresh agent with no other context)

You are reviewing an animated short film. It was made to beat a benchmark film made from the
same creative brief. Be blunt and specific: your job is to say where the benchmark still wins
and what in our film does not look like a finished theatrical 2D anime (Studio Ghibli is the
style reference).

The creative brief, condensed: at sunrise, mist over an abandoned forest; a giant robot has knelt
so long that moss, wildflowers and old trees grow over it; a small bird lands on its shoulder as
the first ray of sun reaches its weathered face; a faint light flickers on inside its chest; the
ground trembles, birds burst from the treetops; with a deep creaking groan the robot slowly rises
as soil, vines and leaves pour from its armor; it lifts its head toward a gap in the canopy; the
engines ignite, sending a great ring of wind across the forest; the robot tears into the golden
sky leaving a curving trail of light and mist as leaves spiral down through sunbeams. It must
look like hand-drawn 2D anime from a finished theatrical film: confident hand-inked lines with
weight variation, traditional cel colouring with hard-edged shadows, hand-painted gouache
backgrounds with real depth, golden sunrise light that unites robot and background, authentic
hand-drawn timing (2s/3s, holds, anticipation), a heavy ancient robot with personality and
readable eyes. It must NOT look like 3D/CGI, vector/SVG art, web animation, AI art, Western
cartoons, or amateur work (stiff poses, empty backgrounds, repeated trees, muddy colours).

Material you get (paths are given in the task message): stills from our film at random times,
per-shot contact sheets, a side-by-side sheet of our film vs the benchmark at matching beats, and
benchmark stills.

Answer with:
1. For each beat in the side-by-side sheet: which is better and why, in one or two sentences.
2. The 10 most damaging problems in our film, most damaging first, each with the timestamp or
   shot, what exactly is wrong, and a concrete fix.
3. Anything in our film that reads as 3D/CG, vector, or AI art, frame by frame.
4. A score out of 10 for both films on: design, composition, line, colour, animation timing
   (as far as stills show it), effects, climax, backgrounds.
