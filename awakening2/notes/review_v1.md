# Independent review: Awakening II v1 vs "The Moss Keeper"

Reviewer: an independent agent, 2026-11-09. I looked at the qa/v1 sheets and the benchmark stills. I also extracted my own frames from both films:
- 1 fps sheets;
- 4 fps strips of every shot;
- runs of consecutive frames;
- full-resolution crops.

I measured timing per frame with a camera-compensated frame difference, and contrast with luma percentiles.

**Bottom line: the benchmark still wins, 7 of the 8 side-by-side beats.**
- The benchmark tells every beat of the brief in order, and its robot is readable in every shot.
- We have better forest paintings and better golden air.
- But our protagonist is a clean red toy whose face, rise and flight are never clearly shown.
- Our climax collapses into an empty plate and a blown-out white blob.

## 2. The 10 most damaging problems (most damaging first)

### 1. The climax never shows the robot tearing into the sky (S14–S16, 55.0–67.5 s)

**What is wrong**
- **S14 launch:**
  - 55.00–55.25: we see the robot lift for six frames, from behind.
  - 55.29–55.33: a 2-frame silhouette flash.
  - 55.38–56.46: over a second of an *empty* forest, with radial speed lines converging on nothing.
- **S15:** the robot is a blob that shrinks to a dot in the reused canopy hole.
- **S16 is one unchanged composition for 8 seconds:**
  - The robot is parked against the top edge, with its top cut off from 61.5 to 63.0.
  - It shrinks from about ¼ of frame height (60.5) to about ⅛ (66.8).
  - From 64.5 a blown-out white exhaust blob swallows its lower half.
- **The brief's "curving trail of light and mist" is missing:** the trail is a straight vertical column of puffs.
- No leaves spiral through sunbeams.

The benchmark holds its robot large in the sky (64.7–70.7) and ends on an S-curved trail.

**Fix**
- **Launch plate:** put the robot in it. Show legs and thrusters leaving the top of frame on a drawn smear, with debris and leaves falling back down through the speed lines; or cut the empty plate to 8 frames or fewer.
- **Rebuild S16 as 2–3 cuts:**
  1. A tracking 3/4 shot with the robot at 40–60 % of frame height, moss and soil streaming off it.
  2. A wide where the trail is a hand-drawn, tapering S-curve across the golden sky: a hot core with a soft painted mist edge, animated on 2s.
  3. The robot as a spark crossing the sun.
- **Exposure:** clamp the bloom so nothing on the robot clips to white, and keep its silhouette darker than the sky.

### 2. The robot does not read as heavy, ancient, overgrown, or with readable eyes (whole film)

**What is wrong**
- **Not ancient.** It is freshly painted red, with a handful of dark chips (S01 7.6, S08, S09).
- **Not overgrown.** The moss is one toon-shaded green cushion repeated dozens of times, sitting on top surfaces like snow caps (S02 11.0, S12 50.25, S13 53.5). There is one sapling, and no roots, grass, vines or flowers growing *out of* the armour.
- **No face in the wides.**
  - The head is sunk between the shoulder pads, so in every wide (S01, S08, S09, S12) there is no face.
  - The eyes only read in S07 (31.75–34.0).
  - From behind (S12, 49.5) the two thrusters read as a second, sad face.
- **Colour jumps from shot to shot:** maroon (S02, S04, S05), bright orange (S07, S10), crimson (S12).
- **The benchmark is readable in every shot:** a round head with visor eyes, ear discs, a chest porthole and a tree on its shoulder, with rust and patina everywhere.

**Fix**
- **Repaint it as old metal:**
  - faded oxide red over grey primer;
  - rust running from every seam;
  - verdigris and dirt in the crevices;
  - lichen spots.

  Keep the red as a worn accent, not the whole body.
- **Replace the cushion moss** with painted clumps that hang over edges, plus grass tufts, ferns, vines, and roots gripping plates. Add a second, larger tree.
- **Open up the face:** raise the head (or drop the shoulder pads) so the face reads in a wide. Give the eyes lids that can move.
- **One colour model per lighting setup** (light, shadow and rim colours), used in every shot.
- **Redesign the back** so the thrusters don't read as eyes.

### 3. The kneel-to-rise beat is never staged (S01, S08–S10, 0–46.5 s)

**What is wrong**
- **It never kneels.** At 7.6 the robot is not kneeling; it stands in a crouch on both feet.
- **S08: no visible rise.** At 34.0 it is already upright, and for 4.5 s it barely changes pose.
- **S09: a quick, stiff straighten.**
  - It straightens from a lean into a stiff vertical column in about a second (39.75–40.75).
  - Dust and soil cover it the whole time, and its head is hidden.
- **None of the craft of a rise:** no anticipation, no push-off, no effort, no overlap, no settle.
- **The "lifts its head toward the gap" beat (S10)** is a dark shape behind a lens flare.

The benchmark is also stiff (11 s of a nearly static standing pose, 37.5–48.5), but it then gives the head-lift as a big, readable close-up (48.5–52.5).

**Fix**
- **Open kneeling:** one knee and one hand on the ground, head bowed.
- **Stage the rise in one readable medium-wide**, from front 3/4:
  - **Anticipation:** it sinks, the shoulders hunch, the hand presses down and the ground cracks.
  - **Push:** two stages, with a stall between them.
  - **Overlap:** the head comes up last; moss and soil slide off late.
  - **Settle:** a held pose, keyed on 3s.
- **Then a close-up** of the head tilting up, with the eyes narrowing at the light.

### 4. 3D camera and render tells everywhere (see section 3)

**What is wrong**
- **3D camera moves.** Moves in these shots are smooth, on ones, with true perspective and parallax change, while the drawings are on 2s, and that mismatch exposes them:
  - S01 1.0–6.5: crane/tilt;
  - S08: push-in;
  - S10;
  - S13: orbit;
  - S16: crane.
- **Timing measure.** About half of our frame transitions fall in runs of consecutive changes, against about 40 % in the benchmark. Most of the benchmark's camera moves step on 2s (30.5–37.5, 54.75–60.4) or 3s (48.5–52.5); only its opening and its flight run on ones.
- **Render tells:**
  - soft-gradient toon shading on the robot's body (chest at 7.6);
  - lens depth of field (S02–S04);
  - lens-flare starbursts (S06, S10, S11, S15);
  - a rigid 3D turn (S12, 49.25–49.75);
  - instanced moss and ferns.

**Fix**
- **Camera:** replace 3D moves with pans and trucks over flat painted levels (parallax only between layers), stepped on 2s.
- **Robot shading:** hard two-tone cel plus a painted highlight.
- **Focus:** painted soft-focus BG layers instead of gaussian DOF.
- **Flares:** remove them all.
- **S12 turn:** animate it as drawn keys with an in-between smear.

### 5. Effects design is wrong for the material (S08–S13)

**What is wrong**
- **Soil looks like wood.** "Soil pouring from its armour" is drawn as brown serrated planks or stakes. At 38.92 (hip and shoulder) they look like wooden posts or chocolate bars; at 40.5 they fan out like splinters.
- **Dust is identical spheres.** Every puff is a cluster of the same sphere ("popcorn"): S08 34.75–36.0, S12 51.5–52.0, the S13 ring.
- **The wind ring is small and too fast.** It is a ring of puffs at garden scale that expands from the robot to the frame edges in about a second (52.0–53.0). No tree bends.
- **Ignition is barely seen:** two small flames for about 1 s (51.0–52.0).

The benchmark sells the ring with a high overhead of the whole canopy (57.5–59.5) and its ignition with several big jets (55.5–56.5).

**Fix**
- **Soil:** clumps that break into falling streams and powder, with drawn smears, 2–3 values, on 2s.
- **Dust:** irregular hand-drawn silhouettes with curling edges, 2–3-tone cel shading, and varied sizes.
- **Ring:** a high wide over the forest. Show a pressure wave flattening and brightening the canopy outward for 2–3 s, throwing up leaves and birds.
- **Ignition:** hold it long enough to read, with flame flicker on 2s, heat shimmer, and debris blown back.

### 6. Visible technical errors (S10, S14, S16)

**What is wrong**
1. **S10, 44.25–46.5:** a flat orange strip runs across the full width at the top of frame. It grows to about 90 px by 46.2: an edge of missing background or a clipping plane.
2. **S16, 66.08–67.5:** a hard vertical seam in the lower-right canopy, drifting from x≈1918 to x≈1870, with offset texture on either side: the edge of a background card.
3. **S14, 55.21–55.25:** hard-edged, dark-brown vertical bars hang from the robot's arms down to the ground. They read as stretched geometry, not a smear.

**Fix**
- **S10:** extend the background or sky plane, or reframe.
- **S16:** widen or overlap the canopy card.
- **S14:** delete the bars, or redraw them as tapered speed lines in the robot's colour that fade out.
- **Before delivery:** add an automated check for constant-colour edge rows/columns and straight seams.

### 7. Shot design: one shot used three times, no establishing vista, the face beat framed out

**What is wrong**
- **The same painting three times.** The canopy hole seen from below, with the same framing, is S06 (27.5–30.5), S11 (46.5–48.5) and S15 (56.5–59.5): 8.5 s of the film.
- **No establishing vista.** There is no shot of mist over the forest at sunrise; the first open sky (horizon and clouds) is at 59.5.
- **The face beat is framed out (S03, 13.5–20).** In "the first ray reaches its weathered face":
  - the top of the head is cropped;
  - the main beam crosses the shoulder and chest, and only the brow ridge warms up (15.5–19.6);
  - the eye socket stays an unlit crescent;
  - the bird is not in the shot.

**Fix**
- **S06:** a wide over the treetops, with birds exploding into the dawn sky.
- **S11:** cut it, or make it the robot's moving point of view.
- **S15:** a side view of the robot breaking through the canopy, with leaves blasting outward.
- **Opening:** add a 4–6 s vista (sky, mist in the low ground, the robot as a mossy hill among the trees).
- **S03:** reframe so the whole face is in frame and the beam crosses the eye.

### 8. The linework is not hand-inked and has no hierarchy

**What is wrong**
- **The robot's lines are thin and almost uniform** (about 1–2 px at 1080p), and its panel lines are fainter still.
- **The moss gets heavier, darker outlines than the robot's own silhouette.** The hierarchy is inverted (S02, S08, S12).
- **Background elements have no line,** while the foreground grass is flat vector blades (S09).
- **Only the S07 face lines taper.**

**Fix**
- **Silhouette:** a heavier, tapered outer line on the robot.
- **Interior:** lighter lines inside the forms.
- **Coloured lines:**
  - colour-traced lines for shadow/light boundaries;
  - colour lines or no line on soft material such as moss and leaves.
- **Weight follows the light:** thinner on the lit side, heavier in shadow.
- **Breaks:** occasional breaks where forms overlap.

### 9. Colour and value: milky, grainy, and the robot sits outside the air

**What is wrong**
- **Milky image.** Haze, grain and bloom flatten everything.
  - The darkest 1 % of pixels averages luma about 47/255 in ours, against about 33 in the benchmark.
  - In S16 (63–66 s), even the darkest 1 % of the frame stays above luma about 100/255.
- **The robot sits outside the air.** In S08 and S09 it stays a saturated dark red while trees at the same depth are pale and hazed, so it looks pasted on rather than lit by the same golden light.
- **Shadows on the red go purple and muddy** (S03 14.0, S04 20.5).

**Fix**
- **Anchor a true dark** in every shot (the foreground, or the robot's core shadow).
- **Grain:** remove it; use a paper texture on the backgrounds only.
- **Bloom:** limit it to the sun and the engine cores.
- **Haze:** apply the same atmospheric haze to the robot as to trees at its depth, plus a warm rim light on the sun side.
- **Shadow colour on red:** a warm deep red-brown, not purple-grey.

### 10. No acting or secondary life in the key moments

**What is wrong**
- **The eye light-up (S07, 31.75–32.0) is a light switch.** The sockets are fixed: no lid movement, no focusing, no head reaction.
- **The ground tremble (S05)** is camera shake, a crack and a few small puffs. Nothing else reacts: not the moss, the flowers, the pebbles or the bird.
- **The bird drops out.** After S07 it isn't readable again until a figure about 50 px tall lands at 70.5–71.5.
- **S17 ends on 5 s of a static hold** plus a 1.7 s fade.

**Fix**
- **Eyes:** the lid opens in 2–3 drawings with a hold, the glow brightens, and the head lifts a few degrees with a creak (on 3s).
- **Tremble:** pebbles hop on 2s, the flowers shiver, and the bird startles.
- **The bird:** let it ride the first jolt and flutter off; land it at a readable size in the ending.
- **Ending:** use part of the hold for the trail in the sky.

## 3. What reads as 3D/CG, vector or AI art, shot by shot

- **S01 (0–8):**
  - The 1.0–6.5 s crane/tilt is a smooth 3D move on ones, with parallax between the trunk layers.
  - At 7.6 the robot's torso has gradient (toon-shader) shading, not cel.
  - The moss is the same outlined sphere instanced over and over.
  - Sparkle particles are evenly spread.
- **S02 (8–13.5):**
  - Dozens of identical green cushions with gradient-to-hard shading read as an instanced 3D mesh.
  - The trunks have gaussian DOF.
  - The helmet is a perfect dome with a perfectly round port.
  - The bird is the one element that reads as drawn.
- **S03 (13.5–20):**
  - The trunks converge in three-point, wide-lens perspective.
  - The beam is an additive gradient shaft with uniform dust particles.
  - The face plate is flat fills with no line variation.
- **S04 (20–25):**
  - The rivets are ruler-perfect hollow circles in perfect rows (vector).
  - The glow (from 22.4) is a flat tan ellipse (vector).
  - The trees have gaussian DOF.
- **S05 (25–27.5):**
  - The legs are flat, untextured maroon planes with 1-px panel lines (vector / low-poly).
  - The camera shakes on every frame (CG).
  - The sky is a smooth gradient.
- **S06, S11, S15 (27.5–30.5, 46.5–48.5, 56.5–59.5):**
  - The canopy from below is clearly a 3D canopy of leaf cards.
  - The lens-flare starburst has thin, regular spikes (photographic/CG).
  - The vines carry identical repeated leaves.
- **S07 (30.5–34):** the least CG shot in the film; only the heavy grain is wrong.
- **S08 (34–38.5):**
  - The robot and the camera both move on ones.
  - The dust is clusters of perfect spheres.
  - The debris is uniform polygon chips (a particle system).
- **S09 (38.5–43):**
  - The soil is "plank" sprites (38.92, 40.5).
  - The foreground grass is flat vector blades.
  - The tree crowns are flattened floating discs.
  - The robot is a smooth 3D slab.
- **S10 (43–46.5):** a CG lens flare, the orange band artefact, and a smooth camera move on ones.
- **S12 (48.5–52):**
  - A rigid 3D turn (49.25–49.75).
  - The trunks are near-identical straight pillars with no taper, roots or branches.
- **S13 (52–55):**
  - The overhead orbit changes perspective every frame.
  - The ferns are one sprite at different scales (instancing).
  - The ring is spheres; the wind streaks are uniform particle lines.
- **S14 (55–56.5):**
  - The brown streak bars (55.21–55.25).
  - The impact frame (55.29–55.33) is a posterised render matte, with star-shaped holes where the flowers are and noise specks. It is not a drawn impact silhouette.
  - The dust mound is a growing sphere cluster.
- **S16 (59.5–67.5): reads as a painterly filter (AI-like):**
  - The small clouds are made of the same leaf-dab texture as the tree crowns, so they look like flying bushes.
  - The mountain is vertical stamped dabs.
  - The canopy is uniform leaf-dab bokeh at every depth, with no painted edges.
  - The bloom-blown core is photographic.
  - The vertical seam at 66.08–67.5.
  - Heavy grain.
- **S17 (67.5–75):** mostly reads as painted. The ground cover is uniform dabs, and the falling leaves are identical outlined ellipses (vector).

**Worth keeping:**
- the forest interiors and golden haze of S01, S09, S12 and S17;
- the bird's landing on 2s (9.25–10.75);
- the stepped chest flicker (21.0–22.9);
- the idea of an impact frame;

Make that imprint readable and put the trail in that sky.
